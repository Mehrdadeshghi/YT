**Title:** Why you hear your name across a loud room 👂
**Alt title:** The cocktail party effect: your brain never stops listening #shorts

**Description:**
A loud, crowded room, and still your own name jumps out. It's called the cocktail party effect; Colin Cherry named the "cocktail party problem" in 1953. One classic theory (Anne Treisman's attenuation model) says your brain turns ignored voices down rather than off, and personally important words like your own name need less to get through. In the classic test, people repeated ("shadowed") a message in one ear while another message played in the other ear: about a third noticed their own name in the ignored ear (Moray 1959; replicated by Wood & Cowan 1995 with 34.6%). The twist: the people who caught their name tended to have lower working-memory capacity, suggesting they were worse at blocking out distraction (Conway, Cowan & Bunting 2001).
