**Title:** How your car knows a tire is low 🛞
**Alt title:** The tire pressure light: a sensor in your wheel, or no sensor at all #shorts

**Description:**
Your car can tell when one tire is low, while you drive. There are two ways.

Direct TPMS: a small battery-powered pressure sensor sits inside each wheel, most often on the inside of the valve, and radios the pressure to the car.
Indirect TPMS: no pressure sensor at all. The car uses the wheel-speed sensors of the anti-lock brakes. An under-inflated tire has a smaller rolling diameter, so it turns a little faster than the others.

In the U.S., new light vehicles sold after September 1, 2007 must have a TPMS (FMVSS 138, after the 2000 TREAD Act that followed the Firestone tire recall, linked to more than 100 deaths). The rule only requires a warning when a tire is 25 percent or more below the recommended cold pressure, so a tire can be low well before the light comes on.
