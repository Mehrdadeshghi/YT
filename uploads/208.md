**Description:**
Atlantic horseshoe crabs have changed little in 445 million years. Their blood carries oxygen with copper (hemocyanin) instead of iron, and it turns dark blue in the air. Their blood cells clot around bacterial endotoxins, even from dead bacteria, so since 1977 the U.S. FDA has approved the Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) test for drugs and devices that touch your blood. Crabs are caught, bled and returned to the ocean, and an estimated 10 to 30 percent die afterwards. A lab-made substitute, recombinant factor C (rFC), has been sold since 2003 and is now an accepted alternative.
