**Title:** Why you forget why you walked into a room 🚪
**Alt title:** The doorway effect: why rooms erase your memory #shorts

**Description:**
You walk into the kitchen and forget why. Psychologists call it the doorway effect. Event-cognition research says your brain cuts experience into "events", and a doorway is an event boundary: when the brain updates to the new event, information from the previous one is pushed out of working memory. In Gabriel Radvansky's experiments, memory dropped after people walked through a doorway, both in virtual rooms and in a real environment, and walking back into the first room did not bring it back (Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin 2011). Older and younger adults were affected about equally (Radvansky, Pettijohn & Kim 2015). Honest caveat: a 2021 study with four experiments could not repeat the effect, except when people's working memory was already loaded (McFadyen et al. 2021).
