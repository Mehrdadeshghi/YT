# #180 OCEAN — The shark that bit U.S. Navy submarines (cookiecutter shark)

**Title:** This tiny shark attacked U.S. Navy submarines 🦈
**Alt title:** The cookiecutter shark: suction lips, saw teeth, perfectly round bites #shorts

**Description:**
In the 1970s, several U.S. Navy submarines had to go back to base: something had bitten the rubber covers of their sonar domes, and the sound-transmitting oil inside leaked out. The Navy first feared an unknown enemy weapon. It was the cookiecutter shark, which grows to only about 17–22 inches. It latches on with suction lips, slices in with a saw-like row of lower teeth, then twists its whole body to cut out a round plug of flesh, about 2 inches across. Its belly glows green, except for a dark collar around the throat, which may look like a small fish from below and lure bigger hunters in. Its round scars are found on whales, seals, dolphins and even great white sharks; off Hawaii, nearly every adult spinner dolphin carries them.
