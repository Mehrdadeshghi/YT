# #163 HOW IT WORKS #12 — Why your phone dies in the cold

**Title:** Why your phone dies in the cold 🥶🔋
**Alt title:** It said 40 %. Then it died. Here's why. #shorts

**Description:**
Your phone said 40 %, then it shut down. It's not broken, it's the cold. In cold temperatures a lithium-ion battery's internal resistance (impedance) goes up. Open an app and the voltage drops further under the load; when it falls too low, the phone shuts itself down to protect its electronics. The good news: it's temporary, warm it up and the battery life comes back. But many lithium-ion batteries can't be charged safely below freezing (32 °F / 0 °C): metallic lithium can plate on the anode and cause an internal short circuit. Apple designs iPhones for 32–95 °F (0–35 °C).
