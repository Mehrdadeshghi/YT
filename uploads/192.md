# #192 WHY? #7 — Why is the sky blue?

**Title:** Why is the sky blue? 🌅
**Alt title:** On the Moon the sky is black at noon. Why is ours blue? #shorts

**Description:**
On the Moon, the sky is black even when the Sun is up: no atmosphere, so no scattering. On Earth it's blue. Three reasons:
1) Air. Sunlight contains every color. Air molecules (nitrogen and oxygen) scatter short wavelengths much more strongly than long ones — Rayleigh scattering goes with 1/wavelength⁴. So blue light comes at you from all regions of the sky, while red mostly passes straight through.
2) Your eyes. Violet is scattered even more than blue, but the scattered light is a blue-and-violet mix, and the human eye responds to it like blue mixed with white: sky blue. (The spectrum of sunlight is the third factor.)
3) Sunset. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight takes a much longer path through the atmosphere. Blue and green are scattered out almost completely, leaving orange and red.
