# #187 WHY? #2 — Why are flamingos pink?

**Title:** Why are flamingos pink? 🦩
**Alt title:** Baby flamingos are grey. So why are adults pink? #shorts

**Description:**
Flamingo chicks hatch grey. The pink comes later, and Wikipedia gives three parts of the answer:
1) You are what you eat. Flamingos filter-feed on brine shrimp and blue-green algae, full of pigments called carotenoids. Liver enzymes break them down into the pigments that colour the feathers.
2) Pink = healthy. A well-fed flamingo is more vibrantly coloured and a more desirable mate; a white or pale flamingo is usually unhealthy or malnourished. In the breeding season, greater flamingos spread carotenoid-rich preen-gland oil over their feathers to enhance their colour, which has been described as applying "make-up".
3) They feed their colour away. Both parents feed the chick crop milk. In the first weeks it is bright red and looks like blood, but it contains no blood cells; the colour comes from the pigment canthaxanthin. Adults feeding chicks become paler.
