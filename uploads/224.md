# #224 How It Works — How adaptive cruise control and lane keeping work (real sky panorama, real asphalt photo, modelled cars)

**Title:** How your car steers and brakes by itself 🚗
**Alt title:** One radar, one camera: how lane keeping and adaptive cruise control really work #shorts

**Description:**
On the highway, some cars steer and brake on their own, with one radar and one camera.

Adaptive cruise control: a radar behind the front bumper sends out radio waves. They bounce off the car ahead, and the time they take to return gives the distance, accurately out to about 525 feet. If that car slows, yours eases off and brakes; when the gap opens, it returns to your set speed.

Lane keeping: a camera behind the windshield looks for the edges of the painted lines. If you start to drift, a small corrective force on the steering turns you back. Systems vary by car: some only warn, others steer.

It only knows what it can see. Faded lines or snow can hide the lane, and in AAA testing, approaching a simulated broken-down car in the lane led to a collision 66 percent of the time, at an average of 25 mph. That's why these are driver assists and your hands stay on the wheel. Speeds, gaps and pulses in the video are illustrations.
