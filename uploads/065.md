Cymothoa exigua, the tongue-eating louse, enters a fish through its gills as a juvenile. It cuts off the blood supply to the tongue until the tongue falls off, then attaches itself to the stub and takes its place. The fish can use it like a prosthetic organ. Females are about 8–29 mm long. In 2005, a red snapper with what could have been C. exigua was found in the UK. It is not harmful to humans.
