# #227 How It Works — How a speed camera knows how fast you're going (real sky panorama, real asphalt photo, modelled car and camera)

**Title:** A speed camera can't see your speedometer. So how does it know? 📸
**Alt title:** How speed cameras measure your speed: the echo trick #shorts

**Description:**
A speed camera never sees your speedometer. Many use radar: a narrow radio beam hits your car and bounces back. If you're driving toward it, the echo returns at a slightly higher frequency, and the faster you go, the bigger the shift. It's the Doppler effect, the same thing that makes a siren sound higher as it comes toward you.

Over the limit, the flash lights up your plate and the calibration lines painted on the road. If the beam hits at an angle, it reads you slower than you really are, never faster, so that error always favors the driver. Other systems skip the beam and time you over a known distance: one mile in 50 seconds is 72 mph. Speeds, distances and the slow motion in the video are illustrations.

This video explains how the technology works, nothing more.
