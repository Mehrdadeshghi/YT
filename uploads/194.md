# #194 WHY? #9 — Why do knuckles crack?

**Title:** Why do knuckles crack? 🤚💥
**Alt title:** Crack once: easy. Crack again right away: nothing. Why? #shorts

**Description:**
Pull a finger and it cracks. Try again right away, and nothing happens. Here's why:
1) A gas bubble. Your joints are filled with synovial fluid, and gases are dissolved in it (mainly carbon dioxide, oxygen and nitrogen). When you pull the joint apart, the pressure inside drops and the gas comes out of solution as a bubble (cavitation).
2) Forming or bursting? For decades the pop was blamed on the bubble collapsing. In 2015, a study saw the bubble still there after the crack, so the sound may come from the bubble forming instead. A 2018 simulation from France argued for collapse again. It's still not settled.
3) The reload. After a crack comes a "refractory period" of about 20 minutes while the gas slowly dissolves back into the fluid. Until then, that joint won't crack again.
Bonus: Does it cause arthritis? There's no scientific evidence for that. American doctor Donald Unger cracked the knuckles of his left hand, but not his right, every day for more than fifty years. Neither hand got arthritis. He published it in 2004 and won the 2009 Ig Nobel Prize in Medicine.
