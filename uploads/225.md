**Title:** Your airbag has 0.04 seconds to save you 💥
**Alt title:** How an airbag works, millisecond by millisecond #shorts

**Description:**
In a crash, an airbag has about four hundredths of a second to do its job.

Tiny acceleration sensors feel the sudden stop, and a control unit decides, typically 15 to 30 milliseconds after impact, whether to fire. An igniter sets off a small charge in the steering wheel; it burns and makes nitrogen gas, and the bag fills in about 20 to 30 milliseconds. The gas is already escaping through vents, so the bag softens as you sink into it. The white dust is a powder like cornstarch or talc that helps the bag unfold.

In the U.S., front airbags must fire in a crash equivalent to hitting a barrier at 14 mph. And never put a rear-facing child seat in front of an active airbag.

The crash clock in the video is an illustration of the published time ranges; real timing depends on the car and the crash.
