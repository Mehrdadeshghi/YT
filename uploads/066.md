Greenland sharks are dated by radiocarbon in the lenses of their eyes. The oldest individual was estimated at 392 ± 120 years, so it was likely born before the USA existed. They grow just 0.5–1 cm a year and only reach maturity at around 150. They can reach 6.4 m and about 1,023 kg. Many carry a parasite on their eyes that leaves them almost blind, yet reindeer, moose and polar bear remains have been found in their stomachs. The meat is toxic unless it is processed, which is how Iceland makes hákarl.
