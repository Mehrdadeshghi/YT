**Alt title:** The coelacanth: the fish that came back from the dead #shorts

**Description:**
The coelacanth was thought to have died out about 65 million years ago, at the end of the Cretaceous. Then, on 22 December 1938, museum curator Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer found one in the catch of the trawler Nerine off South Africa: five feet long, with four limb-like fins. Coelacanths are lobe-finned fish, more closely related to lungfish and four-legged animals (including us) than to ray-finned fish like tuna. Research from 2021 suggests their pregnancy can last up to five years, 1.5 years longer than the frilled shark, the previous record holder. And in September 1997 a second species turned up in a fish market in Manado, Indonesia, spotted by Mark Erdmann and Arnaz Mehta on their honeymoon.
