# #190 WHY? — Why is the ocean salty?

**Title:** Why is the ocean salty? (Rivers aren't) 🌊
**Alt title:** Rivers are fresh. The ocean is salty. Why? #shorts

**Description:**
Seawater is about 3.5% salt, while rivers hold only tiny amounts. Three reasons:
1) Rain. Rainfall washes salt and other minerals out of the ground, and rivers carry them into the sea. Edmond Halley proposed this in 1715 and called it "continental weathering".
2) The sun. Evaporation lifts water out of the sea as vapor and separates it from the salt, so the salt stays behind.
3) Volcanoes. The chloride in sea salt came out of Earth's interior through volcanoes and hydrothermal vents on the seafloor; the sodium leached out of the ocean floor.
Proof you can visit: Utah's Great Salt Lake. Its rivers bring around 1.1 million tons of minerals a year, and it has no outlet besides evaporation, so the minerals accumulate. It is far saltier than seawater.
(The ocean itself is not getting saltier today: its salinity has been stable for billions of years, because about as much salt is removed as is added.)
