**Title:** Why many smoke detectors are radioactive ☢️
**Alt title:** The tiny radioactive source on your ceiling #shorts

**Description:**
Many smoke detectors have a tiny bit of radioactive metal inside. Why?

Ionization smoke detectors use americium-241, about 0.3 micrograms, to fire alpha particles into a small chamber of air. The particles knock electrons off air molecules, so the air carries a tiny current between two electrodes. Smoke particles attach to those ions and the current drops. A circuit compares it with a sealed reference chamber and sounds the alarm.

The idea was found by accident: in the late 1930s, Swiss physicist Walter Jaeger built a poison-gas sensor that didn't work. Then he lit a cigarette and the meter dropped.

It matters: the U.S. National Fire Protection Association reports 0.53 deaths per 100 home fires with working smoke detectors versus 1.18 without (2009–2013).

Not every detector is radioactive: photoelectric (optical) detectors use a light beam and a sensor instead.
