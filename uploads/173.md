# #173 MYSTERY — Nobody pushed these rocks

**Title:** Nobody pushed these rocks 🪨
**Alt title:** Death Valley's sailing stones: the 100-year mystery solved with GPS #shorts

**Description:**
On Racetrack Playa in Death Valley National Park, California, rocks slide across the dry lake bed on their own and leave trails often up to 330 feet long. Some of the stones weigh as much as a person. The trails were studied from the early 1900s on, but nobody had ever seen a rock move. Then scientists put GPS units inside rocks and set up time-lapse cameras, and on December 20, 2013, more than 60 rocks moved at once. The cause: in winter a thin pond forms on the playa and freezes into ice sheets just a few millimeters thick. On sunny mornings the ice breaks up, light wind drives the floating panels, and they shove the rocks along at up to about 16 feet (5 m) a minute.
