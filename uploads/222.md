**Description:**
Some cars can warn the traffic behind them about a jam long before the driver sees it. It's called V2V (vehicle-to-vehicle), or Car2X.

A small radio broadcasts directly from car to car, with no phone call and no cell tower. Up to ten times a second, equipped cars share where they are, how fast they're going and which way. One broadcast reaches roughly a thousand feet, and the standard also allows other cars to pass a message on, hop by hop. The hop chain in this video is an illustration: not every car or system forwards messages, and real range varies.

The catch: it only works if the other car has one too. In December 2016 the U.S. Department of Transportation proposed requiring V2V in new light vehicles. In November 2020 the FCC gave 45 of the 75 MHz in that band to Wi-Fi, leaving 30 MHz for cars.
