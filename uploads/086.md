Between 1801 and 1812, Lord Elgin's agents removed about half of the Parthenon's surviving sculptures. No original of his Ottoman permit has ever been found, and in 2024 Turkey said it knew of no such document. In 1816 the British Parliament bought them for £35,000. In 1937–38 workers scraped them with chisels and carborundum to make them whiter. Greece has requested their return since 1983, and the Acropolis Museum in Athens (2009) shows white casts where London's pieces belong.
The British Museum says Elgin acquired them legally, that around 6 million people a year see them in London, and the British Museum Act 1963 bars it from giving them away. In a 2021 YouGov poll, 59% of Britons said they belong in Greece (18% in Britain).
