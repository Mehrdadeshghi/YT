**Description:**
Before pumpkins, jack-o'-lanterns were carved from root vegetables. In the 19th century, turnips and mangel wurzels were hollowed out and carved with grotesque faces on Halloween in parts of Ireland, Scotland, Wales and England, and some were set on windowsills to keep harmful spirits out. The name comes from strange lights flickering over peat bogs (will-o'-the-wisps), and the Irish legend of Stingy Jack, who tricked even the Devil and was doomed to roam the Earth with only a hollowed turnip to light his way. Immigrants brought the carving tradition to the United States, where it moved to the pumpkin.
