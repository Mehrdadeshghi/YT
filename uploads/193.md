# #193 WHY? #8 — Why does the Moon look huge near the horizon?

**Title:** Why does the Moon look huge near the horizon? 🌕
**Alt title:** The rising Moon is NOT bigger. Try the pebble test. #shorts

**Description:**
The giant Moon on the horizon is an illusion — the Moon illusion.
1) It's not bigger. Its angle in the sky stays the same (measurable with a theodolite), photos at different heights show the same size, and at the horizon the Moon is actually about 1.5% farther away, so it's even slightly smaller.
2) Pebble test: hold a pebble about 0.33 inches wide at arm's length (25 inches). It covers the full Moon high in the sky — and the "huge" Moon on the horizon too.
3) Your brain. The textbook explanation: trees, buildings and land in front of the horizon Moon tell your brain it is far away, so it is perceived as bigger — usually by about 50% in diameter. The sight of the terrain is the most important factor.
The twist: there is still no consensus. "No single theory has emerged victorious" (Ross and Plug), and people have written about it since Aristotle.
