**Description:**
In June 1869, the Brooklyn Bridge's designer John A. Roebling had his foot crushed by a ferry; tetanus killed him the following month. His son Washington Roebling took over as chief engineer until caisson disease ("the bends") left him paralyzed, so he directed "the completion of the bridge through a telescope from his bedroom." His wife Emily Warren Roebling learned the mathematics, catenary curves, strength of materials and cable construction, and for the next 11 years she performed much of the chief engineer's duties, including day-to-day supervision. When the bridge opened on May 24, 1883, Emily was officially the first to cross it.
