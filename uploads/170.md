# #170 OCEAN — Longer than a blue whale

**Title:** This jellyfish is longer than a blue whale 🐋
**Alt title:** The lion's mane jellyfish: 120-ft tentacles, 1,200 of them #shorts

**Description:**
The biggest lion's mane jellyfish ever recorded was measured off Massachusetts in 1865: a bell 7 feet across and tentacles around 120 feet long, longer than a blue whale (up to 98 feet). Some scientists doubt that record, and it may have been a different Cyanea species. Near its mouth, a lion's mane has around 1,200 tentacles with stinging cells; it catches fish, zooplankton and even smaller jellyfish. A torn-off tentacle can keep its sting for up to 25 days. In July 2010, around 150 beachgoers were stung at Wallis Sands State Beach in Rye, New Hampshire, most likely by the remains of a single broken-up jellyfish.
