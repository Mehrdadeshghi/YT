# #184 HOW IT WORKS #13 — Why airplane windows are round

**Title:** Why are airplane windows round? Not why you think ✈️
**Alt title:** Square windows crashed the first jetliner? That's a myth. #shorts

**Description:**
You've probably heard that square windows tore the de Havilland Comet, the world's first jetliner, apart. That's a myth. In 1954 two Comets broke up over the Mediterranean (BOAC Flight 781 off Elba, 35 lives, and South African Airways Flight 201 near Naples, 21 lives). The Comet 1's passenger windows were rectangular with rounded corners, and their shape was not a factor in the accident report. The fatigue crack on G-ALYP started at a tiny rivet hole near the corner of an ADF antenna cut-out in the roof. Every flight pressurises the cabin and stretches the skin; in a water-tank test the Comet G-ALYU burst open after 3,057 pressure cycles. The lesson: stress concentrates where a corner's radius is smallest, and fatigue cracks start at those stress raisers. That's why today's airliner windows have large, rounded corners.
