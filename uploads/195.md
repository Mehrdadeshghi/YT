# #195 WHY? #10 — Why do we get goosebumps?

**Title:** Why do we get goosebumps? 🐈😱
**Alt title:** A scared cat puffs up. You get goosebumps. Same reflex. Why? #shorts

**Description:**
A scared cat puffs up its fur. You get goosebumps. It's the same reflex, called piloerection. Here's why:
1) Tiny muscles. At the base of your hairs sit tiny muscles called arrector pili ("hair erectors"). Your sympathetic nervous system, the fight-or-flight system, makes them contract and pull the hairs straight up. That's the bump.
2) A warm coat. In furry animals, the raised hairs trap air between them. That layer of air insulates and helps the animal keep its heat.
3) Look bigger. Angry or scared animals raise their hair to look bigger and scare off enemies, like frightened cats and chimpanzees. Porcupines raise their quills when threatened, and quills are modified hairs.
In humans, some consider goosebumps a leftover (vestigial) reflex. They still show up with cold, fear, strong emotions, a horror movie, or even music.
