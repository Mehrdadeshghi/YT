# #223 How It Works — Why store security gates beep (real-photo environment + modelled objects)

**Title:** That store gate isn't looking for thieves 🔔
**Alt title:** Why store security gates beep: the strip that won't stop ringing #shorts

**Description:**
Many store security gates don't search for stolen goods. They listen for a tiny strip of metal.

In this common type of gate (acousto-magnetic), a plastic tag holds a thin metal strip, loose enough to vibrate, and a small magnet. The gate fires short bursts at about 58,000 cycles a second, the strip's natural pitch, so it starts to vibrate. Then the gate goes quiet and listens. The strip keeps ringing, and that induces a tiny signal in the gate's antenna. If the frequency and repetition match, the alarm goes off. At checkout a pad demagnetizes the magnet, the strip falls out of tune, and the gate can't hear it any more. A tag that was missed, or one still active from another store, causes the false alarms.

Other stores use other systems, for example radio-frequency tags at 8.2 MHz. This video explains how the technology works, nothing more.
