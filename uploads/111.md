# Wiki Roulette #111 · HOW IT WORKS #3: How a touchscreen works (and why a sausage can do it)
**Title:** Your phone can't feel your finger 👆⚡
**Alt:** Why a sausage works on your phone, but gloves don't 🌭

**Description:**
Would you tap your phone with a sausage? Comment YES or NO 👇

Under the glass of your phone sits an invisible grid of transparent electrodes (usually indium tin oxide). Every crossing of that grid holds a tiny electric field. Your body conducts electricity, so when your finger touches the glass it pulls away part of that field. A controller chip scans all crossings many times per second (typically 120 times or more on modern phones) and calculates exactly where the field dropped. That's why normal gloves don't work: fabric is an insulator. A sausage, on the other hand, is salty and wet, so it conducts enough. In February 2010, during a cold winter in South Korea, people used snack sausages to operate their iPhones, and news reports said sausage sales were soaring. The first finger-driven touchscreen was described by E. A. Johnson of the Royal Radar Establishment in Malvern, England, in 1965, for air traffic control.
