**Description:**
The goblin shark's jaws shoot out up to 9.5 times farther than other sharks'. Two pairs of elastic ligaments are pulled taut like a slingshot; when it bites, they catapult the jaws almost to the tip of its snout. Then it does something never seen in any other shark: it opens and snaps shut a second time in the same strike. This pink "living fossil" is the last of a family about 125 million years old, and in aquariums the longest one survived for one week.
