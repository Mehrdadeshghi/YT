How a fake job interview became part of a $6.3 billion theft. This is the attack chain of the 29 October 2026 Binance hack, based on Binance's own preliminary report of 3 November 2026. The investigation is still running and Binance says independent firms will review its findings, so treat the details as Binance's account.

June: a wallet engineer downloads a tampered Homebrew; Binance catches it and replaces the device, but the hackers learn who runs the wallets. September: another engineer goes through two fake interviews on a real job site; the "assignment" is malware the security tools don't detect. 20 October: an internal AI tool is used to take over two wallet engineers, one of whom holds a container deployment token. 29 October: a container reaches the wallet signing service. 20:05 UTC: 233.46 million XRP leave in four minutes, then SOL, OP and 18,270 BTC. Withdrawals stop, but on-chain EIP-7702 rights keep working, and with MPC wallets there is no single key to revoke them fast. Last transfer 23:56 UTC, delegation cleared 00:10 UTC.

According to Binance, about $6.34 billion was impacted, about $2.85 billion frozen or intercepted, and Binance absorbs the loss. The FBI points to North Korea. North Korea denies it.
