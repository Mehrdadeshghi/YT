Lunchtime in Boston, nineteen nineteen, and the ground starts shaking. Rivets fire out like a machine gun. A steel tank, fifty feet tall, splits open, and two point three million gallons of molasses pour out. A brown wave, twenty-five feet high, rolls down the street at thirty-five miles an hour. It throws a truck into the harbor and knocks a streetcar off its tracks. Then the cold hits it. The syrup turns thick as glue, and the more people struggle, the deeper they sink. Rescuers wade in knee-deep, pulling survivors out by hand. Twenty-one people died. And for decades, on hot summer days, the whole street still smelled of molasses. Could you outrun it?
