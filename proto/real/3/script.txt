You never see lightning strike down. The flash you see shoots up. Deep inside a storm cloud, ice crystals and soft hail collide, and the storm splits its charge. Positive on top, negative below. Then an invisible channel creeps toward the ground, in jumps of about a hundred and forty-eight feet. A spark rises from the tallest tree. They touch. Now the real flash rips upward, at up to a third of the speed of light. Thirty thousand amps. The air turns to plasma. Fifty-four thousand degrees, five times hotter than the surface of the Sun. The air explodes outward. That's the thunder. Forty milliseconds later, it can strike again, through the same channel. That's the flicker. See the flash. Count the seconds, or run?
