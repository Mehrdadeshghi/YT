You're buried twenty-three hundred feet down, and nobody knows you're alive. August fifth, twenty ten. A copper mine in Chile's Atacama Desert caves in. Thirty-three men are trapped, three miles from the entrance. The escape shaft? Its ladders are missing. Their emergency food was meant for two or three days. They stretch it to two weeks. Above them, drills hunt blindly through the rock. Day seventeen. The eighth drill punches into their tunnel. They tape a note to its tip. We are well in the refuge, the thirty-three. Getting them out takes seven more weeks, in a capsule just twenty-one inches wide, built with help from NASA. One by one, they ride it up. The last man out? Their shift boss. Could you have lasted sixty-nine days down there?
